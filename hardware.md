# Silver Lining — Hardware

Silver Lining is designed around a simple but powerful idea: medication support should come to the person, rather than making the person come to a dispenser. Our mechanical system combines four independently operated medicine dispensers, an adjustable pill-release mechanism, and a four-wheel mobile base. Together with an iPad interface and person-following technology, these components turn a stationary storage device into a mobile companion intended to support elderly users' independence.

At the centre of the system are four separate medicine dispensers, each operated by its own MG996R servo motor. Our design uses the nominal 180° positional version with a brushed internal motor. Tower Pro identifies this variant as a metal-geared servo. Each servo will actuate its associated dispensing mechanism between a closed position and a release position, allowing the controller to select a medicine channel without opening the others. The four-channel arrangement provides space for different medicines while keeping their dispensing mechanisms independently controllable.[towerpro]

An important feature is the adjustable finger tab on each dispenser. Pills vary in shape and size, so a fixed opening would not suit every medication. The tab lets us adjust the effective pill-selection space to match the pill being loaded. The aim is to admit and release one pill per dispensing cycle, reducing the chance of multiple pills falling together or an opening being too restrictive to release anything. This makes mechanical adjustment part of the setup process rather than assuming one geometry works for every pill. Reliable single-pill delivery will still require testing with each intended pill type.

The medicine-storage section also incorporates hinged access. This allows a caregiver to open the storage area for refilling, inspection, and adjustment without dismantling the entire robot. Once loading is complete, the cover can be closed for normal operation. This practical detail supports a central design priority: a sophisticated device should remain straightforward for people to maintain.

Below the dispensing assembly, four 360° continuous-rotation servos will drive the base's four wheels. Unlike the dispenser servos, which move to defined angular positions, the wheel actuators are intended for sustained rotation. Our planned steering approach varies the motion of the left and right wheel pairs, allowing the robot to move forward and turn toward its selected user. The base therefore provides the physical link between recognising a person and bringing the dispensing system within convenient reach.

An ESP-based controller will coordinate these actuators, while the iPad's front camera supplies human-detection information. The software is intended to estimate the person's horizontal X offset and forward Z distance, helping determine which direction the base should travel. These camera-derived coordinates are approximate, not guaranteed depth measurements. A wearable BLE tag and receiver add another source of presence information through RSSI, or received signal strength. However, RSSI alone cannot provide dependable direction or guarantee uninterrupted tracking; the intention is to complement vision, not replace it.[bluetooth]

The intended wow moment is the complete interaction: an elderly user engages with the app, the robot identifies a following direction, the mobile base approaches under controlled conditions, and the selected dispenser performs its release cycle. Independent stopping, obstacle protection, and dispensing verification remain essential development requirements. Silver Lining's ambition is not simply to automate a pillbox—it is to bring carefully controlled mechanical assistance closer to the person who needs it.

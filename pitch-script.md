# Q&A CRIB — answers to the guiding questions judges will fire

**Q: Why should you get 10% of the entire fund?**
We're the only pitch that turns fund → policy alignment → measurable outcomes → exportable industry in one motion. $450M remains fully available; we're the down-payment on the age-tech sector the fund was created to start.

**Q: What is your credible deployment pathway in 18 months?**
A working product today (voice, fall escalation, dose events, caregiver console — all live). Phase gates: M0–6 pilot 20 units → M6–12 1,500 households aligned to the HKHA pilot window (2027–28) → M12–18 GDRC homes. Money is milestone-gated, not grant-gated: the $15M deployment tranche releases only after safety gates.

**Q: What measurable impact for Hong Kong residents?**
Three numbers, published quarterly: falls caught (per 100 households); **median minutes from fall to family alert** (target: under 2 minutes, from a current baseline of "nobody knew"); medication adherence via **confirmed dose events** (target ≥85%, with T+15/T+30 re-prompts and a family alert on missed doses). Every metric is already logged by the running system — we demo the trace, not a dashboard mockup.

**Q: Why hardware? Hardware is hard. Isn't software safer to fund?**
Because medication adherence and fall *answering* need a body in the room — a phone app can't hand pills to someone who fell. We de-risk it: $10M spreads the enclosure across HK/GBA contract manufacturers, $8M buys independent safety testing before a single deployed unit, and the $15M deployment is explicitly gated on supplier quotations.

**Q: What's different from fall-detection pendants or mainland robots?**
Three things: we *respond* in Cantonese within seconds of the fall (15 ms decision path, pre-rendered audio that survives connectivity loss); we close the loop with family (photo, address, acknowledged actions); and we're medication-complete (dispensing + confirmed dose events). Pendants detect. We answer.

**Q: Safety, privacy, and what if Ah Bo gets it wrong?**
Vision is advisory — it can escalate, never cancel. Countdown then auto-escalate on silence. False-alarm cost is one info message to family, not an ambulance. Cap of three calls per incident. Data stays in HK, consent-first, family-controlled — funded from the dedicated $2M governance line.

**Q: Elderly adoption — will 80-year-olds use it?**
Zero UI for the elder. She speaks Cantonese, in her dialect's rhythm, to a face — measured at 3.4 s per voice turn with elderly-tuned endpointing (0.8–3.0 s pauses). The only interface her family touches is Telegram, which they already have.

**Q: GBA scaling — why will a HK product work in Guangdong?**
99,600 HK elders already live there and GDRC already runs 26+ homes with 1,200+ HK elders — the customers are pre-assembled. Cantonese-first voice is a moat: Shenzhen's robots speak Mandarin, our users speak Cantonese.

**Q: Why is this a fund-worthy "industry" and not one startup?**
The $10M commissions *multiple* HK/GBA suppliers for enclosures, dispensers, electronics — a supply chain, not a SKU. Training, maintenance and frontline support ($7M) is the services layer. Ah Bo is the first product on an operating system for care — the next products are down-town rental of it.

**Q: What could kill this?**
Safety-gate failure (mitigated: independent testing before deployment money moves), supplier quotes overshooting (mitigated: tranche is quotation-gated), or policy misalignment (mitigated: we ride three existing programmes, we don't need new policy).
